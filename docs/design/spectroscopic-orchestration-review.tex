\begin{abstract}
This document describes the design and implementation of the orchestration layer for
automated spectroscopic follow-up of transients discovered by the RubinAlerts pipeline,
targeting LLAMAS on Magellan/Baade for the MAGNETS collaboration. It covers alert ingestion,
merit scoring, prioritization, scheduling, and multi-program time accounting, including a
cross-night \emph{per-target integration ledger} and \emph{per-program light-curve phase
preferences}. Two principles guide the design throughout: \textbf{no wasted time on sky} and
\textbf{transparent, defensible prioritization} --- an observer should be able to reconstruct
why any target was ranked where it was. The system is exercised by 96 offline unit tests (no
live broker/database calls).
\end{abstract}

\section{Overview}
The orchestrator converts ranked candidates into a LLAMAS observing plan with multi-program
time accounting. It ingests targets from two streams:
\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
  \item \textbf{Alert-driven} --- the RubinAlerts pipeline's \texttt{candidates.csv}
  (merit-ranked SN candidates aggregated across brokers); and
  \item \textbf{Manual} --- a CSV of PI-entered targets (the eventual Google-Sheet front
  end is future work; see \S\ref{sec:future}).
\end{enumerate}
The system is \textbf{LLAMAS-only} (Magellan/Baade; LDSS3 materials in \texttt{ref/} are
reference only). The pipeline of modules is:
\begin{center}
\texttt{normalize} $\rightarrow$ \texttt{prioritizer} $\rightarrow$ \texttt{planner}
$\rightarrow$ \texttt{accounting} + \texttt{target\_ledger} $\rightarrow$ \texttt{output}/\texttt{reporting}
\end{center}

\section{Alert ingestion and liveness}
\subsection{Brokers}
Candidates are aggregated from independent brokers, each with different classifiers, then
cross-matched. ZTF data enters only via ALeRCE-ZTF and ANTARES; there is no standalone ZTF
client.
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}llp{6.7cm}@{}}
\toprule
Broker & Survey & Role \\
\midrule
Fink & Rubin LSST & Primary LSST discovery + SN classification \\
ANTARES & ZTF + Rubin & Galaxy-centric cone search; P(Ia) is a heuristic \emph{proxy} (not ML) \\
ALeRCE-ZTF & ZTF & BHRF forced-photometry classifier \\
ALeRCE-LSST & Rubin LSST & Stamp classifier (cone-search API ignores coordinates; queried globally then filtered locally) \\
ATLAS & --- & Forced-photometry \emph{enrichment} for bright ($<20$ mag) candidates \\
TNS & --- & Cross-match / duplicate \& spectroscopic-confirmation check \\
Rubin RSP/TAP & Rubin LSST & Host-galaxy properties, authoritative photometry \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\subsection{Coverage and the Rubin downtime window}
The southern Deep Drilling Fields (ELAIS-S1, EDFS\_a/b, $\delta \lesssim -32^\circ$) lie
below ZTF/ANTARES/ALeRCE-DB coverage and are therefore \textbf{single-broker by
construction}. This matters operationally: \textbf{Rubin LSST is offline 2026-08-09 through
the end of August 2026}, during which the ZTF-fed brokers are the \emph{primary} alert
source and the southern DDFs lose discovery coverage entirely.

\subsection{Liveness reporting}
A silent broker outage must never be mistaken for a quiet sky --- especially during the
Rubin downtime, when the ZTF-fed brokers are the only source. The aggregator therefore
records, per broker, a status record \verb|{queried, responded, n_returned, error}|, written
to a \texttt{broker\_status.json} sidecar and rendered as a ``Broker Status'' block in the
run report. A broker that raises is recorded as queried-but-unresponsive rather than crashing
the run, so the observer can always distinguish ``no candidates'' from ``broker down''.

\subsection{Cross-matching}
Cross-broker de-duplication uses true angular separation
(\texttt{SkyCoord.separation}) against a fixed radius rather than an RA/Dec box test, whose
effective radius would otherwise shrink with declination (worst in the southern fields).

\section{Merit scoring (alert pipeline)}
The alert pipeline ranks candidates by a \emph{multiplicative} merit, so a candidate must
score well on \emph{every} axis:
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{Merit} = w_{\rm time}\, w_{\rm mag}\, w_{\rm prob}\, w_{\rm host}\,
w_{\rm ext}\, w_{\rm broker}\, w_{\rm moon}\, (w_{\rm salt}\, w_{\rm absmag}).
\end{equation}
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item $w_{\rm time} = \exp(-\Delta t^2 / 2\tau^2)$, $\tau = 10$\,d --- peaks at maximum light.
  \item $w_{\rm mag} = \exp(-((m-m_{\rm opt})/\sigma_m)^2)$, $m_{\rm opt}\approx20.5$ (Magellan S/N sweet spot).
  \item $w_{\rm prob}$ --- \textbf{ML-derived P(Ia) only}. The ANTARES heuristic proxy is
  uncalibrated, so it is kept in a separate \texttt{antares\_proxy\_prob} column and excluded
  from the pooled probability; ANTARES contributes to detection/coverage, not to P(Ia).
  \item $w_{\rm host}, w_{\rm ext}$ --- host-morphology and Galactic-extinction factors.
  \item $w_{\rm broker}$ --- \textbf{coverage-aware}:
  $1 + 0.3\,\mathrm{clip}\!\big(\tfrac{n-1}{n_{\max}-1},0,1\big)$ where $n_{\max}$ is the
  number of brokers that \emph{could} have seen the field given its declination. This ensures
  a single-broker southern field is not penalized relative to a single-broker equatorial
  field; full multi-broker coverage earns up to $+30\%$.
  \item $w_{\rm moon}$ --- folded into the ranking merit (not only into observability
  filtering), so targets close to a bright Moon are correctly down-weighted in the ranking
  itself. Merit is computed with the Moon penalty as a single source of truth, and
  \texttt{candidates.csv} (the orchestrator's input) carries the Moon-aware merit.
\end{itemize}

\section{Prioritization (orchestrator)}
\subsection{Composite score}
Each target receives a composite score that is a \emph{multiplicative science core} plus
two \emph{additive nudges}:
\begin{align}
\texttt{science\_term} &= \texttt{SCIENCE\_SCALE}\times s \times b \times p \times c \\
\texttt{observability\_term} &= \texttt{OBSERVABILITY\_BONUS}\times o \\
\texttt{keyword\_term} &= \texttt{KEYWORD\_BONUS}\times k \\
\texttt{total} &= \texttt{science\_term} + \texttt{observability\_term} + \texttt{keyword\_term}
\end{align}
with \texttt{SCIENCE\_SCALE}$=100$, \texttt{OBSERVABILITY\_BONUS}$=20$,
\texttt{KEYWORD\_BONUS}$=50$. The scales are chosen so the science core ($s,b,p,c\in[0,1]$)
is the dominant axis (a P1 at peak on a healthy budget $\approx100$; a P4 $\approx20$), while
observability and keyword nudges reorder within or across adjacent tiers but cannot by
themselves leapfrog a full priority tier. The phase factor is clamped to $[0,1]$ so it can
never push the science core past its bound. Every target's component \textbf{breakdown is
persisted} (\texttt{score\_breakdown.json}) and printed in the summary, so a PI can
reconstruct any ranking by hand --- where:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}ll@{}}
\toprule
$s$ science & P1--P4 $\rightarrow$ 1.0/0.7/0.4/0.2 \\
$b$ budget & phase-aware factor 1.0/0.5/0.1 (\S\ref{sec:accounting}) \\
$p$ phase & per-program phase preference (\S\ref{sec:phase}) \\
$c$ completeness & per-target integration ledger factor (\S\ref{sec:ledger}) \\
$o$ observability & fraction of night above the airmass limit \\
$k$ keyword adj. & signed boosts/demotions from a controlled tag vocabulary, clamped \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The keyword adjustment $k$ is driven by a \emph{controlled vocabulary} of scheduling tags
(e.g.\ \texttt{urgent}, \texttt{too}, \texttt{near\_peak}, \texttt{backup},
\texttt{low\_priority}), not free-text parsing, so it cannot mis-fire on incidental words in
an observer's notes; the net adjustment is clamped so keywords reorder within or across
adjacent tiers but never leapfrog a full priority tier. A separate \texttt{override} (a.k.a.\
\texttt{mandatory}) tag is a hard, non-negotiable request: such a target bypasses normal
ranking and the Moon-phase eligibility filter, and is \emph{reserved} a slot before the
greedy fill (see \S\ref{sec:sched}). It still cannot defy physics --- a mandatory target that
never clears the airmass limit tonight is reported as unschedulable rather than silently
dropped.

\subsection{Per-program light-curve phase preference}\label{sec:phase}
\textbf{Motivation.} Different programs want a SN at different phases. Cosmology /
standardization science favors \emph{peak} (best S/N, the canonical SALT epoch, and where
luminosity-correlating spectral indicators are measured). Progenitor, circumstellar-material
(``flash''), early-excess, and fast/exotic-transient science favor the \emph{rise} (outer
ejecta, narrow CSM lines that are gone by peak, and transients that evolve too fast to wait).
A single peak-centered weight cannot serve both.

\textbf{Implementation.} The phase factor is computed relative to a per-program
preferred epoch:
\begin{equation}
p = \exp\!\Big(-\frac{(\Delta t - \Delta t_{\rm pref})^2}{2\tau^2}\Big),\qquad
\Delta t_{\rm pref} = \begin{cases} 0 & \text{preference } \texttt{peak} \\ -7\,\text{d} & \text{preference } \texttt{rising} \end{cases}
\end{equation}
with $\tau=10$\,d. Each \texttt{ProgramAllocation} carries a \texttt{phase\_preference}
(\texttt{peak} default; e.g.\ Stubbs/Ia $=$ \texttt{peak}, Villar/exotic $=$ \texttt{rising}
in the example allocations). This requires a \emph{signed} $\Delta t$ (days from peak;
$<0$ rising), threaded from the \texttt{delta\_t} column of \texttt{candidates.csv} or from
\texttt{peak\_mjd} for manual targets. The offset $\Delta t_{\rm pref} = -7$\,d is a tunable
config value; its exact choice is a science-policy decision for the collaboration. Note an
alert-sourced target without a \texttt{delta\_t} value falls back to the legacy symmetric
weight (peak-meaningful only), since the sign is unrecoverable from $w_{\rm time}$ alone.

\subsection{Priority labels}
Merit maps to P1--P4 by \emph{tonight's quartiles}; these are \textbf{within-night relative}
labels, not absolute science classes (a ``P1'' on a quiet night may be weaker than a ``P4''
on a rich one). The summary header states this caveat, and the $<4$-target degenerate case
is guarded by rank-based assignment. Absolute, cross-night-calibrated thresholds are a
natural future refinement once merit statistics across many nights are available.

\section{Scheduling}\label{sec:sched}
Targets flagged \texttt{mandatory} (\S\ref{sec:phase}) are placed first in a reservation
pass --- each is given its best (lowest-airmass) slot within its observable window --- and the
greedy fill then works around those reserved blocks. For the remaining targets, the greedy
scheduler steps through the night, at each slot selecting the observable target maximizing
\begin{equation}
\text{score} - 10\times\text{airmass} - \texttt{SLEW\_PENALTY}\times\theta_{\rm slew},
\end{equation}
where $\theta_{\rm slew}$ is the angular distance from the previously scheduled target
(\texttt{SLEW\_PENALTY}$=0.5$). The slew term is a modest tie-breaker: the IFU's 1-minute
overhead model assumes negligible slew, which is reasonable for clustered DDF targets, and
this term guards against large cross-sky jumps between consecutive picks. The scoring mode
(\texttt{prioritizer} vs.\ \texttt{fallback/priority-only}) is stamped into the summary
header so it is always clear which ranking produced the plan. Standard stars are placed at
start and end \emph{and} interleaved at a configurable mid-night cadence
(\texttt{standard\_interleave\_hours}$=3.5$) for spectrophotometric calibration over a long
night. Exposures are split into sub-exposures of at most
\texttt{max\_single\_exposure\_sec}$=900$\,s for cosmic-ray mitigation.

\section{Time accounting}\label{sec:accounting}
Per-program budgets are defined per moon phase (dark/grey/bright) in \texttt{allocations.yaml}.
Time is \textbf{charged on schedule} and trued-up post-night by \texttt{reconcile}.
Key properties:
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item \textbf{Consistent state path.} \texttt{run-nightly} and \texttt{reconcile} derive
  the state file from the same \texttt{output\_dir/time\_accounting.json} convention (helper
  \texttt{\_default\_state\_path}), so a reconciliation always updates the night it belongs to
  rather than charging against an empty ledger.
  \item \textbf{Science-time charging.} The budget is charged \texttt{charged\_minutes}
  (exposure $+$ overhead), not the padded wall-clock window; gap-fill / end-of-night stretch
  is tracked separately as \texttt{padding\_minutes} for transparency but never billed.
  \item \textbf{Phase-aware budget factor.} The 1.0/0.5/0.1 budget factor uses remaining
  hours \emph{in the relevant moon phase} as a fraction of that phase's allocation
  ($>50\%\rightarrow1.0$, $>0\rightarrow0.5$, exhausted $\rightarrow0.1$), so dark time is
  not throttled because grey time is short, and vice versa.
\end{itemize}

\section{Per-target integration ledger}\label{sec:ledger}
A cross-night ledger (\texttt{target\_ledger.json}, stored beside the accounting state)
answers a question the per-program budgets cannot: \emph{does this target already have enough
integration time that we should stop re-observing it?} Without it, five consecutive nights
could keep re-scheduling the same high-merit object.

\subsection{Identity, completeness, and remaining-time scheduling}
Targets are keyed by sky coordinate (matched within $2''$ angular separation, so a name
change between nights --- e.g.\ an internal alert ID later upgraded to a TNS/IAU name --- is
still recognized as the same object; names are tracked as aliases with the TNS name
preferred as canonical). For each target:
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item \texttt{completeness\_fraction} $=$ cumulative science seconds $/$ required seconds,
  where the required exposure is \emph{recomputed every night} from the current
  magnitude/redshift --- so a fading SN correctly needs more time.
  \item A target is \textbf{satisfied} (excluded from scheduling) at fraction $\ge0.95$ or
  when the remaining time is below a worthwhile $15$-minute block.
  \item The completeness factor folded into the score is
  $\max(0.15,\,1-\text{fraction})$, dropping to $0$ when fully done. The $0.15$ floor
  prevents the opposite failure mode --- a target getting one short exposure and then being
  dropped forever --- while the taper prevents endless re-picking.
  \item A partially-observed target is scheduled for only its \emph{remaining} needed time.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Phase-split integration count}
The cumulative count is \textbf{split by phase bucket} derived from the observed $\Delta t$:
\texttt{rising} ($\Delta t < -5$\,d), \texttt{peak} ($|\Delta t|\le5$\,d), \texttt{declining}
($\Delta t > 5$\,d), or \texttt{all} when $\Delta t$ is unknown
(\texttt{phase\_bucket\_window\_days}$=5$). Completeness is judged against the bucket the SN
is in \emph{that night}, so a target that is ``done at peak'' can still be scheduled for
needed rising-phase time, and vice versa. The ledger JSON format migrates old scalar records
into the \texttt{all} bucket automatically.

\subsection{Multi-group alert}
When a single sky object is associated with more than one program --- whether from two
same-position requests on one night or from a program already recorded against that object on
a prior night --- the system emits an alert: a \texttt{logger.warning}, a
``Multi-group Targets'' section in the summary, and a \texttt{multi\_group\_alerts.json}
sidecar with schema \texttt{\{name, ra\_deg, dec\_deg, programs, phase\_preferences,
observed\_phase, same\_phase\}}. The \texttt{same\_phase} flag tells PIs whether the
groups want the object at the same phase (one observation may serve both) or different phases
(coordination / separate observations needed). Conflict \emph{arbitration} is intentionally
left to the PIs for now (see \S\ref{sec:future}).

\section{Exposure estimation}
The orchestrator estimates LLAMAS exposure via a cascade: a redshift$\rightarrow$time table
from the proposal (\textbf{linearly interpolated} so there are no discontinuities between
tabulated rows, and clamped outside the tabulated range), falling back to magnitude scaling
(mag 20 $\approx$ 45\,min, $2.5\times$ per magnitude), then a 45-minute default. The IFU
overhead is $\sim$1\,min (vs.\ $\sim$10\,min for slit instruments). Moon/airmass scaling is
intentionally omitted here, since the proposal table is the reference for LLAMAS exposures;
this is a deliberate divergence from the alert-pipeline estimator, not an inconsistency.

\section{CLI and outputs}
\begin{lstlisting}[language=bash]
python -m orchestrator plan          --date ... --targets t.csv --moon grey
python -m orchestrator run-nightly   --date ... --candidates c.csv \
        --allocations a.yaml --moon grey --output-dir out/ \
        [--target-ledger out/target_ledger.json]
python -m orchestrator reconcile        --program P --actual-hours 3.5 ...
python -m orchestrator reconcile-target --name "SN 2026abc" --actual-minutes 90 \
        [--phase peak] --date ...
python -m orchestrator ledger        --target-ledger out/target_ledger.json \
        [--show-satisfied]
\end{lstlisting}
Transparency artifacts written per run: the timeline, TCS catalog, and summary; plus
\texttt{score\_breakdown.json} (per-target score components), \texttt{time\_accounting.json}
(budget audit), \texttt{target\_ledger.json} (per-phase integration), \texttt{broker\_status.json}
(ingestion liveness), and \texttt{multi\_group\_alerts.json}.

\section{Testing}
The system has 96 unit tests (\texttt{tests/}). A hard rule: \textbf{no test contacts a live
broker, database, or API} --- brokers are stubbed and all state uses temp-file JSON.
Coverage spans merit (moon folding, coverage-aware broker bonus), ingestion (liveness status,
ML-only probability, angular-separation dedup), accounting (state path, science-time
charging, phase-aware budget factor), prioritization (normalized score, breakdown invariant,
slew penalty, small-list guard), exposure interpolation, standards insertion, the integration
ledger (completeness, phase splitting, migration), phase preferences, and multi-group alerts.

\section{Open questions and future work}\label{sec:future}
\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
  \item \textbf{Manual-request front end.} A Google-Sheet ingester (gspread + service
  account) is planned so PIs can enqueue targets directly; the CSV path is the current
  stand-in. See \S\ref{sec:sheet} for the interface spec.
  \item \textbf{Phase-preference tuning.} The \texttt{rising} offset ($-7$\,d), $\tau$, and
  the phase-bucket window ($5$\,d) are science-policy values to be set with Chris and
  Ashley's group; per-program offsets (not just \texttt{peak}/\texttt{rising}) are a natural
  extension.
  \item \textbf{Multi-group conflict arbitration.} Today we alert; a future option is to
  model an object wanted at two phases as two distinct requests, each with its own ledger
  bucket and required time.
  \item \textbf{Absolute priority thresholds.} Replace within-night quartile P-labels with
  absolute merit thresholds once cross-night merit statistics are available.
  \item \textbf{S/N-based exposure and completeness.} The exposure estimate is currently a
  static cascade that omits airmass, sky/Moon brightness, and seeing, and the ledger's
  completeness is measured in integrated \emph{time}. The principled target is a proper
  exposure-time calculator (with airmass-scaled extinction and sky, Moon brightness, and
  seeing terms) and, ultimately, completeness judged from the \emph{achieved} signal-to-noise
  rather than elapsed clock time. The ledger is already forward-compatible: cumulative time
  can be swapped for cumulative S/N$^2$ (which adds linearly under background-limited
  conditions) with no change to its structure.
  \item \textbf{Multi-night look-ahead} scheduling, and surfacing broker liveness through the
  \texttt{supernova\_monitor} pipeline path (currently only the \texttt{run\_tonight} path).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Planned: Google-Sheet manual-request front end}\label{sec:sheet}
The intended interface for PI-entered targets (not yet implemented) reads a Google Sheet via
\texttt{gspread} with a read-only service account; rows map onto the same \texttt{Target}
schema the manual-CSV path already accepts (with the optional \texttt{program},
\texttt{phase\_weight}/\texttt{peak\_mjd}, and \texttt{delta\_t} columns described above):
\begin{lstlisting}[language=Python]
import gspread
from google.oauth2.service_account import Credentials

SCOPES = ['https://www.googleapis.com/auth/spreadsheets.readonly']

def fetch_sheet(sheet_id: str, range_name: str) -> list[dict]:
    creds = Credentials.from_service_account_file('credentials.json', scopes=SCOPES)
    client = gspread.authorize(creds)
    sheet = client.open_by_key(sheet_id)
    worksheet = sheet.worksheet(range_name)
    return worksheet.get_all_records()
\end{lstlisting}
An open design question is status feedback --- how to mark targets ``observed'' back in the
sheet once they are scheduled/satisfied.
